Right quote,
wrong conclusion

A proposed causal metric for detecting intrinsic hallucination: the case where a language model quotes the source correctly but draws a conclusion it never supports.

Ayesha Zafar

The idea in one line

An honest conclusion needs its evidence to stand; a hallucinated one does not, so we remove the evidence inside the model and measure which conclusions fall.

01

The problem

When a language model summarizes a document, it sometimes copies a real fact correctly but stretches it into a claim the source never made. The fact is present, even attended to, so the output looks grounded, yet the inference is wrong.

Source: “sales rose in one store.”  →  Summary: “sales rose company-wide.”
The quote is real; the conclusion is an over-reach.

This is an intrinsic hallucination: an inference error, not a fabricated fact. It recurs in a few shapes:

  • Over-generalization: one case presented as all cases.
  • Causal reversal: “A caused B” becomes “B caused A”.
  • Scope / quantity shift: “some” becomes “most”.
  • False certainty: “may” becomes “does”.
  • Wrong attribution: a claim tied to the wrong agent or time.
02

The gap

Existing metrics ask a version of “is the fact supported?”, which is answerable by surface correlation. They catch coarse errors but degrade on subtle inference. Specifically they miss:

  • Subtle inference errors: the quote matches, so overlap and entailment checks pass the over-reach.
  • Distributed evidence: support spread across non-adjacent sentences.
  • Surface shortcuts: detectors often ride dataset artifacts and collapse out-of-distribution (Dubanowska et al., EMNLP 2025). Our error has no surface cue.
  • The gray zone: over-reach is a matter of degree, so labels themselves get unreliable (VeriGray, 2025).

03

Clinical relevance

The method is domain-independent, but clinical summarization is where this error does the most damage and where catching it is most useful. Discharge notes, referral letters and care-record summaries are read as if they were the record itself, so a conclusion that quietly overshoots its evidence can change what a clinician believes about a patient. In most applications an imperfect answer is annoying; in a care record, “almost right” can be a safety event.

Record: a symptom is noted at a single visit.  →  Summary: “the patient has an ongoing condition.”
The symptom is in the record. Nothing is fabricated. The evidence is real; the conclusion overshoots it, and the clinical meaning changes.

The same shapes recur across clinical text: a discontinued medication reported as ongoing (temporal), a one-off finding read as a persistent condition (scope), a relative’s history attributed to the patient (attribution). These are exactly the cases surface and entailment checks wave through, because the quote is present.

What clinical use demands

Per-claim
the exact unsupported statement must be flagged so a clinician can act on it, not just one overall score
Meaning-sensitive
it must catch errors that change clinical meaning while passing surface and entailment checks
Reference-free
it must work at deployment, where no gold summary exists for a clinician to compare against

The approach that follows is built to meet exactly these demands, and its clinical instantiation runs through the plan: a longitudinal benchmark slice (medication timelines, discontinued-then-referenced events, cross-record contradictions), expert-in-the-loop clinical usefulness ratings, and a Finnish and English cross-lingual extension.

04

The approach: a causal metric

Instead of asking “is the fact present?”, the metric asks “does the model’s endorsement of the conclusion causally depend on the evidence?” A licensed inference collapses when its evidence is removed; an over-reach does not, because it never truly rested on it.

new the causal grounding-dependence test: ablate the specific evidence, use the endorsement drop as the score, plus the controlled minimal-pair benchmark.   borrowed claim decomposition, extractive/inferential routing, multi-hop gathering, observer probe, VeriGray labels, conformal abstention, activation-patching tooling.

05

Novelty & honest positioning

Scoped against what already exists, not around it:

  • ReDeeP (ICLR 2025) detects RAG hallucination from a correlational internal reliance signal. Our distinction is a genuine ablation with the endorsement drop as the score.
  • ContextFocus (2026) & “the knob” (2024) already steer generation along a context-reliance direction, so steering-based mitigation is largely pre-empted, and the direction’s existence is established (good for feasibility, not novelty).
  • The sharpest wedge: all prior work targets knowledge conflict, the model ignoring context. Our error is the opposite: the model uses the quote but reasons wrongly from it. A “was the context attended?” signal passes our case, and we have not found work that targets this failure mode.

The central risk, stated plainly

The property that makes this case distinctive may also make activation methods fail on it: if the evidence is genuinely used, a “how much does it depend on the evidence?” signal could look identical for a fair conclusion and an over-reach. Whether the signal separates them is an open question, which is why the plan front-loads a falsification pilot.

The hardest case is false certainty. When “possible signs of cognitive decline” becomes “has cognitive decline”, the stretched claim leans entirely on the real text, so hiding the evidence may lower both versions equally. For that shape, a complementary claim-side test holds the evidence fixed and changes the claim instead: if the model endorses the unhedged version as strongly as the hedged one, it is not tracking what its evidence actually supports.

The most durable contributions are the benchmark and the error-type framing.

The plan: six potential papers

These are proposed papers, not finished work. The structure keeps the floor papers standing regardless of the core bet, and marks the upside papers that depend on it, so a defensible baseline (the survey, the benchmark, and the robustness study, plus a pivot paper) survives even a negative result.

07

Validation & kill criteria

  • Falsification pilot (go/no-go, first): ~200 hand-built pairs across the error shapes, including hedged/unhedged pairs for the claim-side test. Does either signal separate fair from stretched? If not, pivot rather than build on a broken premise.
  • Head-to-head: balanced accuracy / AUROC on the inference subtype plus human correlation, vs all baselines under a shared reader model.
  • Robustness: ignore meaning-preserving paraphrase; react to a minimal meaning-flip.
  • Out-of-distribution transfer: hold above chance across datasets, separating understanding from artifacts.
  • Real-error validation: confirm on naturally occurring over-reach, not only synthetic pairs.
  • Ablations: prove the causal test does the work, not the borrowed scaffolding.
08

Indicative timeline

Core (Phases 0 to 4) is roughly 9 to 11 months of clean work, or about 12 to 15 months for a strong single paper with slippage, and 18 to 24 months for the full four-to-six paper arc. Submit the survey and benchmark early while the metric is built. The remaining time extends the metric to real Finnish care-record summaries with clinician review, the setting that motivated it.
